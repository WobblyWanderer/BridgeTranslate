# BridgeTranslate: first user journey

This document defines the crossing before any hosting platform or programming framework is chosen.

## Purpose

BridgeTranslate is a universal communication bridge.

It is particularly useful when natural, nonlinear, neurodivergent, disabled, dyslexic, hyperlexic, alexithymic, non-speaking or multilingual communication meets a linear system such as the NHS, benefits, local government, housing, employment, education or formal complaints.

The user remains the owner of the meaning. The bridge helps map, check and format it.

---

## Screen 1: Welcome

### Main message

> Say it your way. Add anything that helps explain it. BridgeTranslate helps turn your meaning into a form another person or system can understand.

### Supporting message

- You do not need a diagnosis.
- You do not need to organise your thoughts first.
- Speaking is not required. The whole crossing can be completed in writing.
- You may use a Context / About Me document to explain communication barriers or access needs.
- You decide whether the bridge understood you correctly before any final document is produced.

- **Start crossing**
- **How this works**
- **Privacy and AI processing**

## Screen 3: What would help the bridge understand you?

Diagnosis is optional. Users choose communication patterns using large icon-led cards.

Each card contains:

1. a plain description of the lived experience;
2. an optional explanatory label in brackets for people who already know the reason or terminology;
3. one short example.

The bracketed labels are navigation aids, not diagnoses.

### Thinking and information routing

- 🕸️ **My thoughts arrive connected** *(gestalt / networked thinking)*  
  I may explain several linked things before the main point becomes visible.

- 🔎 **I know what I mean but cannot find the word** *(word retrieval / dysnomia)*  
  I may describe a person, object or role by its function, story or relationship.

- 🌀 **My information comes out in a different order** *(sequencing / executive function)*  
  Please organise it without removing useful context.

- 📚 **I understand complex ideas but forms confuse me** *(hyperlexia / institutional translation)*  
  I may understand the subject while not understanding what the question expects.

- ⏳ **I need time before the full explanation arrives** *(delayed processing)*  
  Important context may become available after I have started answering.

- 🧱 **I understand better through examples, pictures or practical systems** *(concrete or visual processing)*  
  Abstract wording may need a real example or structural map.

### Feelings, body signals and stress

- 🌫️ **Feelings are hard to identify or name** *(alexithymia)*  
  I may know something matters before I can label the emotion.

- 🌡️ **Body signals are hard to identify or explain** *(interoception)*  
  I may notice a change in function before I can describe the sensation.

- ⚡ **Stress changes how well I can communicate** *(processing or regulation load)*  
  I may lose words, speech, memory or sequencing when overloaded.

### Speech and written communication

- ✍️ **I communicate only in writing** *(non-speaking / written communication)*  
  Do not require a voice response or telephone conversation.

- 🔇 **My speech may stop or become unreliable** *(situational speech loss / intermittent speech)*  
  Writing may remain available when speaking is not.

- 💬 **I use a communication aid or another person to support communication** *(AAC / supported communication)*  
  Accept typed, selected, symbol-based or supported input as my own communication.

- 🎙️ **Speaking is easier than typing** *(speech-first input)*  
  Let me dictate naturally and correct the written result afterwards.

- ⌨️ **Typing is easier than speaking** *(text-first input)*  
  Keep every stage available without voice input.

### Reading and language

- 🔤 **Reading dense text is difficult** *(dyslexia / visual or language processing)*  
  Use short sections, clear spacing and optional explanations.

- 🌍 **English is not my first or strongest language** *(English as an additional language)*  
  Use clear English and avoid unnecessary idioms.

- 🧭 **I understand the words but not what the system wants** *(pragmatic or institutional translation)*  
  Explain the function of the question before asking me to answer it.

- ❓ **I am not sure** *(unknown / exploring / undiagnosed)*  
  Help me identify which support patterns fit without requiring a label.

### Optional identity or diagnostic labels

Users may additionally select labels they already use, including:

- Autism
- ADHD
- AuDHD
- Dyslexia
- Hyperlexia
- Alexithymia
- Dyspraxia
- Aphasia or word-finding difficulty
- Non-speaking
- AAC user
- English as an additional language
- Other / exploring / undiagnosed

These labels must never be required to use the bridge and must never override the communication patterns selected by the user.

## Screen 4: Add context and evidence

Two clearly separated upload areas prevent context from being mistaken for evidence.

### A. Context / About Me

Optional material about:

- communication style;
- disability or access needs;
- relevant background;
- recurring barriers;
- preferred language or terminology;
- communication methods, including written-only or AAC communication.

This material helps interpretation. It is not automatically treated as proof of a specific event.

### B. Documents about what happened

The user may add whatever is relevant, including:

- letters;
- forms;
- emails;
- screenshots;
- reports;
- notes;
- photographs of paperwork;
- existing timelines.

No arbitrary product-level file limit is defined here. Each implementation must test real user material and explain any unavoidable technical boundary honestly.

### Actions

- **Add Context / About Me**
- **Add documents or screenshots**
- **Continue without files**

## Screen 5: Say it your way

### Main prompt

> Tell the bridge what happened, what you are trying to explain, or what you need help saying.

### Guidance

> Fragments, repetition, tangents, emotion and unfinished thoughts are allowed. Do not organise it first.

### Inputs

- large text box;
- speech input where supported and chosen;
- paste existing notes;
- AAC or supported input where available;
- optional question: **What outcome do you need?**

The user must never be required to speak.

### Actions

- **Map what I mean**
- **Back to documents**

---

## Screen 6: Meaning check

The bridge presents a plain-language map before drafting anything formal.

### Suggested structure

- **What happened**
- **Why it matters**
- **What you want the other person or organisation to understand**
- **What outcome you appear to be asking for**
- **Important dates, people or evidence found**
- **Anything uncertain or missing**

### Main question

> Is this what you are trying to say or describe?

### Actions

- **Yes, that is my meaning**
- **Not quite, let me edit it**
- **Ask me a question in writing**
- **Go back and add more**

The bridge must not proceed to a polished final document until the user confirms or edits this meaning map.

- 🏠 **Housing**
- 💼 **Employer or reasonable adjustments**

The user may choose more than one output where the same confirmed meaning needs different forms.

## Screen 8: Draft check

The bridge produces the chosen format and asks:

> How is this?

The result should be editable on screen.

### Actions

- **Change the tone**
- **Make it shorter**
- **Add more detail**
- **Keep more of my own voice**
- **Make it more formal**
- **Show what came from which document**
- **Edit it myself**
- **Yes, you bridged the gap**

The system must preserve the confirmed meaning and must not invent dates, facts, diagnoses, legal duties or evidence.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. The user owns the meaning.
2. Diagnosis and speech are never required.
3. The bridge accepts natural input before asking for linear structure.
4. Context and evidence remain distinguishable.
5. The bridge reflects meaning back before drafting.
6. Uncertainty is shown, not guessed away.
7. The final result remains editable.
8. The peer version does not require a permanent account history.
9. The reference specification remains platform-neutral.
10. Organisations adopting the bridge remain responsible for their own hosting, security, retention and legal obligations.
