# Accessibility should be infrastructure

BridgeTranslate exists because many people can explain what happened perfectly well in their own language, but public systems demand a narrow, linear, institutional format before they will listen.

That mismatch is routinely treated as an individual failure. It is not. It is a design failure.

Government departments, councils, NHS bodies, benefits agencies, housing providers and other public institutions should build their own in-house translation systems so users can:

- explain events naturally, by voice or text
- upload relevant documents and screenshots
- receive plain-language explanations of questions and decisions
- organise evidence into a chronology
- see how their words have been interpreted
- correct mistakes before submission
- download an accessible copy of the final record
- use the service without needing a diagnosis first

These systems should be co-designed and tested with neurodivergent, disabled and multilingual users.

BridgeTranslate is a prototype, a stopgap and a proof of concept. It is not permission for institutions to keep publishing inaccessible forms while disabled people build private workarounds in the ruins.

Not everyone has the time, money, energy, technical confidence or concentrated nerd-density required to build a personal translation system while already dealing with illness, disability, poverty, care responsibilities or institutional conflict. 🤓✋️

Accessibility should be part of the service itself, not an unpaid engineering project assigned to the person asking for help.
